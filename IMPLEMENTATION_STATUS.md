This is an audit against the separately provided `PLAN.md`, not a claim of feature completeness.

The normal Crystal specs use checked-in Rust-generated single/multi-track fixtures, indexed raw/DEFLATE round-trips, a headless `d4-plot` sampler test, and loopback HTTP Range integration. `crystal spec --single-module`, Crystal formatting, and Ameba are required CI checks. `bench/memory.cr` measures GC allocation: on this Crystal 1.21.1 environment, one versus two million alternating secondary records allocated about 117/121 kB while writing and 390/400 kB while reading, with GC heap size under 1 MiB after reading. `bench/speed.cr` provides release-build wall-clock medians and GC allocations for primary-only sequential scan and 1/2/4-worker aggregation. `bench/index.cr` measures repeated random reads: 200,000 alternating secondary records, 32 points near the end, warm median about 131 ms unindexed versus 4.7 ms indexed on this environment (about 28x). The initial cross-language comparison with Rust 0.3.11 exposed expensive per-value spooling, point-reader allocation, and bit-level decoding. After optimization, warm medians on the same fixtures improved by about 4–6x for writes, 2.5–3.3x for uncompressed scans, and 1.7x for compressed scans; full numbers and methodology are in the companion optimization report. Indexed files now work in both directions on the tested Rust 0.3.11 build. Approximately 1 GB and 2 GB synthetic primary and secondary files were scanned and written by both implementations; Rust file mappings dominate RSS on the primary scan. Actual 10 GB WGS workloads and cache-cold I/O remain to be tested. None of the unsupported milestones should be inferred from a passing round-trip test.
